01 · PROBLEM

Most swarm research assumes a world that doesn't exist.

After a disaster, drone swarms could search collapsed structures faster than any human team. But academic multi-agent RL usually assumes perfect communication, perfect sensing, and infinite battery — assumptions that collapse the moment a swarm leaves the lab.

Swarm-SAR asks a harder question: how do decentralized swarms cooperate when communication is lossy, sensors are noisy, energy is finite, and hardware fails mid-mission?

02 · SYSTEM

Four pillars, one framework.

POLICY

Transformer + GNN, trained CTDE

A Transformer handles intra-agent reasoning; a Graph Neural Network passes messages over the live communication graph — so coordination degrades gracefully as links drop. Centralized training, fully decentralized execution. Scales 3 → 10 drones.

SIMULATOR

A new disaster every episode

Procedurally generated maps — buildings, fire and smoke, no-fly zones, dynamic obstacles, victims — force generalization instead of memorization. Noisy camera/GPS/LiDAR, a battery model, comms with range, latency, and packet loss, plus hardware-fault injection.

METRIC

The Swarm Intelligence Score

A novel composite metric — a weighted geometric mean of coverage, rescue, energy, comms, and safety. Its method ranking stays stable under weight perturbation (Spearman ρ = 0.96), so conclusions don't depend on arbitrary weight choices.

EVALUATION

Rigor by construction

Benchmarked on a frozen map suite against frontier exploration, Lloyd/Voronoi coverage control, greedy-TSP, and a privileged oracle — reporting IQM with bootstrap confidence intervals (rliable-style), not just mean ± std.

Communication needs a controlled evaluation.

In the 20 October 2026 heuristic reference run, mean rescues were 6.35/8 at 0% packet loss, 6.00/8 at 30% loss, and 6.75/8 with communication disabled (20 maps per condition). The disabled condition also changes radio range and peer visibility; this does not establish a causal communication effect. These are self-contained simulation results, not learned-policy or hardware validation.

The previous SIS 25-to-72 comparison used a retired activity-based metric and has been removed. The next research milestone is to isolate message content in frozen learned policies and reconcile historical checkpoint evaluations.

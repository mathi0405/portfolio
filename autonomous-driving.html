01 · RESEARCH QUESTION

A learned policy vs. forty years of control theory.

Can a deep RL agent, trained entirely from simulation, match or surpass hand-engineered classical controllers on structured driving tasks?

The agent must hold its lane, take curves at varying curvature, regulate speed to a target, and avoid obstacles — with no rules encoded, operating from camera pixels alone while the classical baselines enjoy privileged state.

03 · REWARD DESIGN

Behavior, decomposed.

rt = wv·speed + wp·progress − wl·lane − wh·heading − ws·steer − wj·jerk + terminal
+ speedtrack the target velocity + progressarc-length advancement along route − lane errorlateral deviation from centerline − heading errormisalignment with road tangent − steer effort / jerksmoothness, no twitchy control terminallarge penalty on collision / off-road

Every sub-reward is logged independently in training telemetry — when the agent misbehaves, the responsible term is visible immediately instead of buried in a scalar.

PPO's clipped surrogate objective made it the primary algorithm for its stability on continuous control; SAC's maximum-entropy formulation serves as the off-policy comparison point.

The checked-in results use state observations in the lightweight fallback environment. PPO succeeds in 19 of 20 episodes; SAC succeeds in 17. These numbers do not establish camera-only CARLA performance.

Both classical baselines achieve 20/20 successes in this configuration. Performance in CARLA, on unseen routes, or under sensor degradation requires separate evaluation.
